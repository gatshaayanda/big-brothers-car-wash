## Walk-in queue: required end-to-end workflow
- Intake form fields: customer's full name; phone number; vehicle type (e.g. sedan, SUV, bakkie); optional vehicle registration; service (default to the configured general walk-in service); optional notes.
- Primary submit label: **Join the queue**. Validate required fields and phone number reasonably; explain validation inline. Prevent accidental duplicate submissions and provide a clear reference/status after successful server persistence.
- Staff can manage queue order and wait-time estimates, update job/queue status, and mark jobs as started and completed. Wait estimates must be based on real staff-entered/configured data or clearly labelled estimates, never fabricated promises.
- Clearly distinguish a local/offline draft from a server-accepted queue entry. Never tell staff a customer has joined the shared queue until the server confirms persistence. Retry with a stable request ID where supported to avoid duplicates.
- The staff phone handoff is a privacy-sensitive shared-device flow: do not expose the previous customer's personal details, account, receipt or notifications to the next customer. After submission, provide a clear safe hand-back/finish state; clear transient form state and do not silently authenticate the customer as the staff member.

## Customer identity, accounts and future bookings
- The queue is the entry point to a durable customer relationship, not a dead-end form. Let customers join as guests without friction, then offer a clear way to secure/claim their customer account using their phone and a verified sign-in/linking method. Do not equate a typed phone number with verified ownership.
- Preserve a guest's queue/job history when linking an account where the auth provider supports safe identity linking. Do not create duplicate customer records or lose records during linking; use stable customer IDs and verified ownership checks.
- Customers should be able to return to their own job history, receipts, notification preferences and future bookings. Enforce customer ownership on the server/Firestore rules; never expose another customer's records through a guessed ID, phone number or URL.
- Offer future appointments after intake and in the completed-job/receipt journey, without distracting from the primary walk-in action. Customers can request a slot only from admin-configured opening hours and slots that are genuinely available.
- Admins manage opening hours, service durations/capacity and bookable slots through real CRUD. Prevent double-booking with server-side transactional/authoritative availability checks. A submitted booking is **requested/pending** until the business confirms it; never call it confirmed merely because a form submitted.
- Do not add payments or imply payment was taken unless a real verified payment integration exists.

## Big Brothers Racing — family fun, fair rewards, and offline-first practice
- Product framing: the game is **Big Brothers Racing**, a free, optional driving-practice game that works as a quick break while waiting for a wash and as a reason to revisit the app at home. Keep the wash queue/booking action primary; the game is a supporting experience, not a gate to service.
- Preserve the approved racing physics, 3D scene, assets, controls, touch ergonomics, crash/pit behavior and existing route. Branding/copy and clearly scoped reward/account additions are allowed; do not change the driving model or control layout without explicit owner authorization and separate QA.
- Family-friendly positioning: invite a parent, sibling, cousin or friend to take turns, practice, compare personal bests and talk about technique. Avoid manipulative streaks, guilt, pay-to-win mechanics, wagering, loot boxes, or pressure to play longer. The goal is enjoyable skill-building and shared fun, not screen-time maximization. Avoid promising child-development outcomes.
- Small optional rewards can have real local value: the owner has proposed P10/P20 discounts, comparable to a small tuckshop snack or airtime/reboost. These are **not currently guaranteed or automatically awarded by game performance**. Do not advertise a discount as active, generate redeemable vouchers, or count an offline score as a verified award unless an authenticated admin-configured offer and server-side eligibility/redemption workflow actually exists. Future offers should define amount, qualifying performance, start/end dates, stock/usage cap, one-per-customer rules, minimum spend if any, and redemption confirmation; admin can disable them. A real-world discount is a bonus, never a reason to pressure children or make purchases necessary.
- Existing racing career may store local/offline records. Clearly distinguish device-only history and virtual credits from server-verified results. Do not promote local/offline scoreboards as shared or trusted. Server owns eligibility, official leaderboard results, reward issuance and redemption. If online multiplayer/family races are added later, use server-authoritative sessions/results, safe display aliases, rate limiting and moderation; never imply multiplayer exists today.
- Offline-first acceptance: the customer should be able to reopen the cached game route, load the game shell and required local racing assets, play solo, pause/resume, restart, and retain clearly labelled device-local progress without a connection. Cache versioning and updates must preserve the install mechanism; do not change the protected install card, beforeinstallprompt lifecycle, PWA registration, install UX or unrelated shell cache behavior. Test a fresh online visit then airplane-mode reopen, gameplay, reload, browser storage failure, and recovery/re-sync when connectivity returns. Keep service-worker fetch handlers resilient to a failed optional remote import.
- Future roadmap (not claims of current capability): (1) admin-controlled reward offers and server-validated redemption, (2) storylines and missions with local progress, (3) online friends/family races and shared leaderboards. Build in small verified increments, preserve solo offline practice, and make online features degrade gracefully when data runs out.
- Research-informed UX: gamification evidence supports autonomy, competence and relatedness, but average effects are modest and poorly designed public rankings can discourage less skilled players. Favor personal-best improvement, clear immediate feedback, forgiving difficulty and optional co-play over a single harsh public ranking. Sources: systematic review of gamification theories https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2021.106963; meta-analysis of gamification and autonomy/relatedness https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7; family co-play study https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444818767667. Offline design should use service-worker cached shell/assets and clearly label unsynced data; background sync is opportunistic, not guaranteed. Source: MDN offline/background operation https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Guides/Offline_and_background_operation.
